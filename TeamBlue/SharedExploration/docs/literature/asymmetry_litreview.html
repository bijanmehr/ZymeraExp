Breaking symmetry in a parameter-shared coverage swarm

Why identical agents flood instead of dividing — and the mechanisms, training and inference-time, that fix it.

Consolidated from two structured literature sweeps (12 domain scouts, adversarial verification) and our own inference-time probes on the frozen 24²/6 checkpoints · rooms/open-trained LPAC-CNN+GNN policy · decentralized obstacle-grid coverage under a soft connectivity objective.

Abstract. A parameter-shared CNN+GNN coverage policy applied per agent is permutation-equivariant end to end, so under determinism identical, co-located agents provably emit identical actions and cover the same cells — the flooding we measure as a redundancy floor ≥ 2.2. This is a theorem with two independent causes (an expressiveness gap and a credit/coupling gap), not a hyperparameter to tune. Breaking it needs an asymmetry source (unique IDs or randomness, per Angluin) plus a way for the frozen policy to execute the asymmetry. That second requirement is decisive and empirical: because the policy navigates by a radius-1 local frontier, edits to what it perceives can repel but cannot attract across a gap, so they only disperse. Mechanisms that physically move each agent into a complementary region are the only ones that produce true division. We rank the training-time and inference-time options against these constraints, fold in our probe results, and note that the winning wrapper is standard multi-robot practice, not a contribution.

1The flood is a theorem, not a bug

The stack is permutation-equivariant: a per-agent CNN → global-average-pool → a GNN knowledge-base whose normalized-max aggregator sits at or below the 1-Weisfeiler–Leman expressiveness limit → a shared goal head, one 160,869-parameter network vmapped across agents. Two clustered agents with automorphic communication neighbourhoods and identical CNN features therefore receive provably identical fused beliefs z_i, choose identical goals, and overlap by construction [1,2]. The redundancy floor has two independent causes that must both be paid:

  • Expressiveness gap. The network cannot even represent different actions for two symmetric agents — a 1-WL limitation, independent of the reward.
  • Credit / coupling gap. From a symmetric state the exact per-agent difference reward D_i is itself symmetric — both overlappers score ≈ 0 — so it can ratchet a split once one exists but cannot initiate or allocate one.

Two theorems frame the fix. Angluin's impossibility [3]: in an anonymous network, symmetry cannot be broken deterministically — unique IDs or randomness are required, which is exactly what justifies our unique-ID assumption. And the load-bearing distinction throughout: diversity ≠ division. Making agents distinguishable (a label, a random tag) is necessary but not sufficient; making them complementary (a partition that tiles the space) is the actual goal, and only mechanisms that couple agents' choices or assign disjoint regions deliver it.

2Two axes that organize everything

Axis A — when it acts: a training-time mechanism changes the weights (the network learns to use an asymmetry); an inference-time mechanism is a wrapper on the frozen policy (no weight change).

Axis B — can the frozen policy execute it? This is the decisive filter we measured, and it sorts every inference-time mechanism into three tiers:

TierWhat it doesResultExamples
Tier 1Physically moves agents into complementary regions, or supplies a live local gradient every steptrue divisionregion deployment (DARP / seq-greedy), Voronoi–Lloyd descent, graph dispersion

The filter is subtle: Angluin guarantees you can create asymmetry; the real question is whether a radius-1 frozen navigator can act on it. That single criterion demotes the entire perception-edit family and elevates deployment.

Theory takeaway. Only coupling (rank 1) has an output that is complementarity-by-construction, certified to (1−1/e) for monotone-submodular coverage [7]. Identity tags and position make division representable; D_i selects the useful break; a committing goal head holds it — but the coupling round is what turns diffusion into division. For submodular coverage the "When Is Diversity Rewarded" theorem [13] shows heterogeneity is already optimal under the right aggregator, so an added diversity bonus is largely redundant — which is why rank 5 is demoted.

Adversarial caveats (verification pass) The rank-1 pitch overstates its warrant on three points: (i) the "exact submodular D_i" is only truly submodular at cover_r=1 — at cover_r=0 coverage is one-cell-per-agent, i.e. modular, and the per-sector claim-conditioned marginal the rank-mask needs is not what the code computes; (ii) the (1−1/e) bound is for sequential greedy, but mp_rounds=2 is a parallel masked relaxation, and a clustered start is a near-complete conflict clique that needs sequential tiers ≈ its clique-cover number, not a fixed 2; (iii) priority must be temporally persistent (position or a stable scalar) — a per-step random priority reshuffles the assignment every step and collapses back to diffusion. Rank 2's i/N risks being index-relabeling whose cross-N allocation is unproven at a held-out team size. Rank 3 is an anchor, not a causer — it is weakest exactly at the clustered onset where division must originate. Net: rank-1 is the right class; the guarantee is the weaker parallel-greedy bound and must be validated empirically at cover_r=1.

Best candidate. One-shot region deployment: run the ID-ordered obstacle-aware partition once while the team is fully connected at t=0 (the only moment the global snapshot exists), physically deploy each agent to its region via a go-to-seed step or Lloyd descent (both supply the live local gradient the frozen policy lacks), then hand navigation back to the unchanged weights inside a contiguous territory where its own frontier is again live. It uses exactly our assets (one snapshot + unique IDs), produces certified division, and generalizes because regions are defined as fractions of N and of the free-cell mass.

Adversarial caveats (verification pass) Deployment seeds division; it does not maintain it. Three checkpoint-specific attacks: (i) the frozen weights are region-blind and were trained under a global-λ₂ connectivity drive, so after handback at a spread-out layout the learned connectivity term actively pulls agents back across territory borders — redundancy can rebound. Durable division may need a deploy + far-field-mask leash (which we confirmed repels reliably), i.e. the real mechanism is deploy+mask, not deploy alone. (ii) The "certified obstacle-aware DARP" is not computable at t=0: only a small gossiped patch of the occupancy map exists then, so the partition degrades to geometric equal-area Voronoi on an optimistic (unknown = free) grid, and seeds can straddle walls or land in disconnected pockets. (iii) A transit tax: driving ~10 clustered agents to spread seeds burns ≈ 15–30% of the 100-step horizon, and the god-view per-step re-Voronoi oracle (which is strictly stronger) already caps at ≈ 72% raw / ≈ 45% decentralized at 32²/10 — so a static t=0 assignment cannot exceed that ceiling and pays transit on top. Durable division must be measured, not assumed.

6Novelty assessment

As a mechanism, the winning wrapper is standard multi-robot practice, not a contribution. Composing a frozen local navigator with a classical global allocator — DARP, Voronoi/Lloyd, CBBA, distributed submodular greedy — is the textbook hierarchical pattern in multi-robot exploration; the learned-policy analog is LPAC [33], the size-invariant coverage backbone this policy already resembles. This is consistent with the program's own architecture-positioning verdict. The only defensible novelty is framing: (a) the negative result that far-field perception edits provably cannot steer a radius-1 frozen shared policy while deployment can — which motivates the allocator-in-front pattern specifically as symmetry-breaking for a parameter-shared learned policy that otherwise floods; and (b) using the once-available t=0 connected snapshot as the exact and only window for the global partition. The wrapper is engineering scaffolding; the survivable novelty in this program remains the covert-adversary substrate and the coverage/connectivity Pareto-ceiling.

7Verdict & recommended experiments

Inference-only (no retrain). The frozen ceiling is a deployment mechanism: partition + physically move + release. A cheaper same-day probe is decode-time distributed sequential-greedy goal arbitration over the frozen 9-compass goal-logits — the one inference lever that imposes complementarity externally rather than hoping the symmetric net emits it. Everything that needs the net to use an asymmetry (identity channel, position-grounding) needs at least warm-start retraining.

The experiment to run: a 2-phase rollout on the frozen open/rooms checkpoints at 16²/4 and 32²/10 — (A) frozen-from-clustered baseline; (B) one-shot equal-area Voronoi deploy (optimistic grid) → release; (C) deploy + far-field-mask leash; (D) god-view per-step re-Voronoi ceiling. Log the redundancy floor, %-of-optimal coverage, and inter-agent distance across the handback boundary. The mechanism is real iff redundancy drops toward 1 and stays below baseline through step 100 while %-of-optimal ≥ baseline despite the transit tax. If redundancy rebounds after handback, arm (C) is required and "deploy alone" is falsified.

If a retrain is on the table: a learned rank-masked coupling round inside the mp_rounds KB, trained under the submodular D_i with the goal head holding the split and position-grounding ON — no discrete role heads, no standalone diversity bonuses (redundant with submodularity).

8Open questions

  • Does the frozen policy expose any goal/waypoint input, or must a separate go-to-seed controller sit in front of it? (Determines whether deployment is drop-in.)
  • How much does a static t=0 partition degrade when regions turn out wall-blocked or agents are lost after the graph fragments — is a local region-steal / re-auction-on-reconnect rule needed?
  • What fraction of the horizon is the deployment transit tax at small grids / short horizons — does it erase the coverage benefit?
  • After deployment, is a single contiguous local frontier inside a sub-region actually in-distribution for a policy trained on the clustered multi-agent frontier?
  • Once choices are coupled, does D_i still cleanly separate "redundant-but-blue" from a covert red agent — or does coupling confound the contribution-as-detector signal the resilience story depends on?
