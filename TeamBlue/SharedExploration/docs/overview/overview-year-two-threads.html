Era 0 · designing the team

The toy

grid_env · train_ippo · visualizer — one file

It began with one grid and one question: can agents cover ground together? The first cut was a single agent.

What I got wrong

Started single-agent. A lone agent isn't the subject — what emerges between agents is.

Era 1 · both threads, born together

RedWithinBlue

GridCommEnv · CTDE · a red/blue ladder

The adversary thread is born here — and born tangled with the team. Nominal agents are blue; the adversary is red, and it is one of the team, not an outsider at the gate.

Decision

Model the threat as an insider that passes every local check. The enemy is trust, not a wall — the choice that sets the whole program.

Era 2 · designing the team · adversary simmering

Learning to hold together

zymera_env · a JAX swarm engine · connectivity-aware coverage

Agents learn to cover an unknown world while staying in touch over a limited-range, moving graph. The formalism sharpens: a micro agent, a bridge that couples them, a macro mission.

micro ati ~ πi(·|hti)   bridge Gt=(I,Et)   macro J(π)=𝔼[Σ R]
Finding

Coverage and connectivity trade off, and the wall is method-independent. Connectivity — λ₂(L(Gt)) — is set by the reward, not the architecture.

Decision

No hard connectivity mask. A mask makes the graph unbreakable — which would quietly end the adversary thread, since a team that can't fracture has no resilience to study. Keep it soft.

Era 3 · designing the team

The architecture, and a detour

zymera_lab · a multi-level policy · a distilled planner

The agent grows three levels: decide a goal, plan a route, take a step. A learned planner is distilled from an exact wavefront to generalise to unseen maps.

What I got wrong

Ran a 72-run tournament to pick the best learned planner. They came out interchangeable — the question was wrong.

Finding

The planner has an exact ground truth. Don't approximate it — use it. A deterministic planner generalises perfectly, for free.

Era 4 · both threads, converging

The adversary comes home

covert-adversary missions · two prongs

The team is mature; the adversary rejoins it. The two threads braid again — a covert insider on a decentralised team with no dispatcher to fall back on. The contribution has two sides, and they were always the two threads: designing the team and the adversary within.

On hold · revisit-if

Collective transport fails the comms-coupling test as a physical task — revisit if reframed as heading-consensus transport, where the team must agree a direction over comms.
