Zymera · policy architecture & training memory

Every layer, every parameter, and where the 50 GB actually comes from

The real trained model (introspected, not sketched): the perception–action–communication policy, the CTDE critic, the frozen planner, and why 32² blows past a 49 GB GPU.

LPAC backbone · depth 2 · width 64 · mp_rounds 2 · feedforward (no BPTT) · obs = 7 channels · 206,374 params total

The one-paragraph version. Each agent sees a 7×H×W local picture. A tiny CNN (2 conv layers, 64 channels) turns it into a 64-number feature; a GNN fuses that with in-range neighbours over the comm graph into a 64-number belief. Off that belief hang small heads — a 9-way goal head (L3), a 2-way role head, and value/λ̂₂ readouts. The chosen goal goes to a frozen 19-parameter MVProp planner (L2) that floods a value field, and a parameterless argmax picks the move (L1). A separate CTDE critic (used only in training) scores the joint state. Total learnable weights: 206 K — but only ~133 K are active (the GRU + GCRN recurrent modules are built but off). The weights are tiny; the memory is all activations, and that's what scales with the grid.

Takeaway: the network is tiny — 206 K weights, less than a megabyte. Nothing here is memory-heavy by itself. So the 50 GB is not the weights; it's what the weights produce during a training step.

Where the 50 GB comes from — a wide batch, not a deep chain

There is no backprop-through-time: recurrence is feedforward, so each step's forward depends only on that step's observation (the GRU is built but its hidden state stays zero). Instead, one PPO update re-runs the actor + critic forward over the entire batch at once and differentiates it in a single backward pass:

16 rollouts × 100 steps × N agents = 16,000 agent-forwards (at N=10)  — all differentiated together

Every intermediate activation of all 16,000 forwards must be held simultaneously for that backward. The dominant term is the CNN feature maps, which carry the grid:

activation bytes ≈ rollouts × horizon × N × (grid²) × width × layers × 4

The jump is grid² × N: (1024/576) × (10/6) ≈ 1.8 × 1.7 ≈ 3×. That single ~3× is the whole reason 32² needs the big GPU. (The 73 GB you see on balthar is mostly XLA pre-grabbing ~75% of its 98 GB — the true peak is ~50 GB.)

How to fit 32² on the 49 GB GPU — three levers, none touching the 16 rollouts

leverwhat it doeschanges the numbers?
minibatch the losssteps are independent (feedforward) → backprop the 16×100 in chunks, accumulate the gradient → peak ÷ #chunksno — identical gradient

bf16 activationsstore activations in 2 bytes not 4 → ~halves them (keep λ̂₂ eigendecomp + advantages in fp32)yes — needs a validation run

Because it's a wide feedforward batch, not a temporal chain, the first two are the clean fixes — they change what is stored vs recomputed, not a single number, so no re-validation is needed. The recurrent training path in this codebase already minibatches over episodes; the feedforward path just doesn't yet. bf16 is the fallback if a numerical change is acceptable.

Introspected from a trained checkpoint (`runs/mvprop/rooms24_mvprop_occ/model.eqx`): exact per-module parameter counts and shapes via jax.tree_util over the Equinox modules. Param counts are grid-independent (convolutional/GNN → size-invariant); activation memory is grid-dependent, which is the entire point. Memory figures are order-of-magnitude estimates from the dominant CNN-activation + stored-trajectory terms; the true peak includes GNN activations, the CTDE critic, optimizer state, and XLA workspace. "Inert" modules are built for a stable parameter surface but carry no gradient in the feedforward + goal_head configuration these runs use.
