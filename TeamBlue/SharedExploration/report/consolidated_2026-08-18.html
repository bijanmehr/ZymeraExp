Read this first — the metric. Coverage = 1×1 stood-on (the cell each agent operates on). The agent senses a 3×3 window, but that's navigation range, not the deliverable. Earlier numbers I quoted as "56–66% coverage" were the inflated 3×3 SENSED figure — the true numbers below are ~1.5× lower. Connectivity = one-piece (fraction of ticks the team is a single connected component); giant = mean share of agents in the largest component.

The story in three lines. (1) A connected 10-agent chain physically caps coverage — the connectivity tax. (2) Difference credit is the division-of-labor fix — it roughly doubles coverage (21→44% stood-on) but trades connectivity (99→78%). (3) Tuning the reach weight trades along that frontier; reach 3 is the reliable knee (~38.5% / 92%). Neither reach-weighting nor the full guardrail stack has cracked strict 99% one-piece — ~92% (giant 98–99%) looks like a real ceiling for this team.

Where we stand

Operating-point candidate: reach 3 ≈ 38.5% stood-on / 92% one-piece / 98% giant (guardrails-off); the guardrail-on equivalent is the same ~38/90, so guardrails aren't buying coverage.

The ceiling: two independent methods (reach-weighting & the guardrail stack) both stall at ~92% one-piece — but giant is 98–99% everywhere, so it's never a real split, just ≤1 agent briefly detaching. Strict 99%-of-ticks-one-component is the unmet bar; "essentially connected" is met robustly.

The fork: (1) accept ~38/92 (giant ≈99%) and move to the red study; (2) redefine to delivered-coverage / intermittent connectivity (allow brief disconnection, require periodic reconnect — the cleaner research story); (3) change physics (more agents / larger comm_r).
