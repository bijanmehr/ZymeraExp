Cooperative Exploration on Rooms Terrain

Five studies, three team scales, collision-free — and a transfer problem the numbers make unavoidable.

60 training runs (T2·18 + T3·15 + T4·27), 3 seeds each · rooms/corridors terrain · 100-step horizon · cover_r=0 · every rollout verified collision-free (max same-cell overlap = 0). Coverage is raw % of free cells (god's-eye ceiling ≈ 72% at 32²). Numbers are final-50-iter means ± std across seeds.

The problem — skills do not transfer, and behaviour is not reproducible

Two of the program's stated goals are generalization across scale and reproducible behaviour. The results contradict both, and it is worth showing the evidence before anything else.

ReadingThe size-invariant backbone, the role split, and the count-invariant critic were each meant to make a skill learned once hold up as the team and world grow. On rooms terrain they do not: performance roughly halves from 16² to 32², the trained value estimate does not transfer, and seed-to-seed variance is large. Whatever the policies learn is scale-specific and seed-specific — closer to memorized behaviour for one setting than a transferable skill.
