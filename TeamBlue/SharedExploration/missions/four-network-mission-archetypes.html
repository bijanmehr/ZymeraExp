Four Network-Mission Archetypes

Zymera · candidate second mission · verified sources

Four ways a mobile team can make the network the mission

Each panel is a small live simulation in the paper’s own terms — mobile agents, range-limited links that form and break. The network can carry, compute, spread, or be. Papers below are real and verified.

✓

The test each one passes. A mission differs from your shared-exploration work only if you could have perfect coverage and still totally fail at it.

A · the network carries

Flow

produces → throughput / QoS

Task agents move; a few mobile relays reposition to keep multiple flows connected over range-limited links. Where two flows share a relay → congestion (the min-cut bottleneck).

Not coverage: you can see everything and carry zero flow, or carry everything while blind to the map.

  • Mobile Wireless Network Infrastructure on Demand · Mox, Calvo-Fullana, Gerasimenko, Fink, Kumar, Ribeiro — ICRA 2020 · arXiv:2002.03026the anchor: UAVs reposition to route multiple data flows for a moving task team
  • A Networked Multi-Agent System for Mobile Wireless Infrastructure on Demand · Calvo-Fullana et al. — 2023 · arXiv:2306.08737the deployable system; reconfigures for QoS, “resilient to agent failures”
  • Navigation with Shadow Prices to Optimize Multi-Commodity Flow Rates · 2023 · arXiv:2309.14284multi-commodity flow with mobile relays, made literal
B · the network computes

Agreement

produces → a shared heading

A moving flock aligns on a shared heading through local exchange — consensus you can watch in the motion; a misaligned agent (red) is ignored (resilient). λ₂ sets the speed.

Not coverage: you can observe everything and still converge on a wrong or inconsistent shared belief.

  • Resilient Flocking for Mobile Robot Teams · Saulnier, Saldaña, Prorok, Pappas, Kumar — IEEE RA-L 2017real robots agree on a heading with faulty / malicious members
  • Resilient Average Consensus: A Detection and Compensation Approach · 2022 · arXiv:2202.10814spot and compensate corrupted values during consensus
  • Byzantine Resilience at Swarm Scale · 2023 · arXiv:2301.06977how consensus resilience scales with team size
C · the network spreads

Spread

produces → information reach

On a moving network, the message spreads by encounters — an informed node meets an uninformed one and passes it on — so reach grows over time.

Not coverage: you can be everywhere in space and still fail to relay the message to a partitioned node.

  • Collaborative Information Dissemination with Graph-based Multi-Agent RL · Galliera et al. — 2023 · arXiv:2308.16198agents learn to disseminate on dynamic networks; scored on reach + overhead
  • Dynamic Graph Communication for Decentralised Multi-Agent RL · 2025 · arXiv:2501.00165communicating over a changing graph
  • Graph Neural Network Meets Multi-Agent RL (survey) · 2024 · arXiv:2404.04898the map of GNN-based multi-agent communication
D · the network is

Structure

produces → the topology itself

Robots spread toward goals while a decentralized rule keeps the graph connected — links stretch to the range limit (turning red near breaking), then hold. λ₂ stays positive.

Not coverage: but pure structure with nothing flowing over it is under-motivated — usually wrap it around A, B, or C.

  • Decentralized Global Connectivity Maintenance for Multi-Robot Navigation (RL) · Li, Jie, Kong, Cheng — ICRA 2022 · arXiv:2109.08536learned policy navigates while holding global connectivity
  • Decentralized Multi-Robot Line-of-Sight Connectivity Maintenance under Uncertainty · 2024 · arXiv:2406.12802hold LoS links despite localization noise
  • Connectivity Maintenance and Recovery for Multi-Robot Motion Planning · 2025 · arXiv:2510.03504maintain and recover connectivity while moving
